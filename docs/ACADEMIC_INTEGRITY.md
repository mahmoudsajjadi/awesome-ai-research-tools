# Academic Integrity, Ethics & Publisher Policies

Guidelines for ethical integration of AI tools in peer-reviewed scientific publishing.

---

## 1. Publisher Policy Consensus (ACM, IEEE, Elsevier, Nature, Springer)
- **Authorship:** AI systems cannot be listed as authors on scholarly papers because authorship implies legal accountability for scientific veracity.
- **Mandatory Disclosure:** Most premier venues require explicit disclosure of AI tool usage in an acknowledgment section or designated *Declaration of Generative AI* statement.
- **Factual Responsibility:** The human authors remain fully responsible for the accuracy of equations, mathematical proofs, experimental results, and literature citations.

---

## 2. Standard Publisher Disclosure Template
When submitting manuscripts to venues requiring AI transparency, adapt this template:

```latex
\section*{Declaration of Generative AI and AI-Assisted Technologies}
During the preparation of this work, the author(s) used [Tool/System] for [e.g., language editing, grammar checking, LaTeX formatting]. After using this service, the author(s) reviewed and edited the content as needed and take full responsibility for the content of the published article.
```

---

## 3. Avoiding Hallucinated Citations
- Always verify digital object identifiers (DOIs) and publication volumes for references suggested by AI models.
- Cross-check citations against authoritative databases such as PubMed, IEEE Xplore, Google Scholar, or ACM Digital Library before submitting camera-ready versions.
